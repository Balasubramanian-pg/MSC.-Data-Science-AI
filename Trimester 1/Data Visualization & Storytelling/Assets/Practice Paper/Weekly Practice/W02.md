## Quiz Set 1: Displaying Data & Chart Types

### Question 1
**When should you use simple text to display data?**

- **When you want to focus attention on a single, important number.**
- When you are presenting a large, complex dataset.
- When you need to show the relationship between many variables.
- When you need to compare multiple categories.

---

### Question 2
**What is the primary function of a heatmap?**

- To interact with our verbal system so the audience reads the data.
- To show the precise values in a large dataset.
- **To use color saturation to guide the brain to points of interest.**
- To display a trend over a continuous time series.

---

### Question 3
**What type of chart is most effective for showing the relationship between two quantitative variables?**

- **Scatter plot**
- Waterfall chart
- Line chart
- Horizontal bar chart

---

### Question 4
**When designing a table, what is a key principle to follow?**

- Make the table data-centric so the data stands out.
- **Use different colors for every other row to improve readability.**
- Use heavy borders to clearly separate all cells.
- Ensure the audience must read the table to understand it.

---

### Question 5
**Which visual is best suited for showing a starting value, the positive and negative changes, and the final resulting value?**

- Stacked bar chart
- Slopegraph
- Square area chart
- **Waterfall chart**

---

### Question 6
**Why are vertical bar charts often preferred over other chart types for comparing the magnitude of different categories?**

- They can handle negative values, unlike pie charts.
- They are the only charts that can show a time-series relationship.
- **The bars have a zero baseline, which allows for accurate visual comparison.**
- Their bars must always be ordered in ascending or descending value.

## Quiz Set 2: Intentional Design & Storytelling

### Question 7
**What is the main principle of "intentional design" in data visualization?**

- **Ensuring every element, like color and alignment, serves a clear purpose.**
- Using as many colors as possible to make the visual appealing.
- Making the chart complex to show the depth of the data.
- Tailoring visuals for a single, generic audience type.

---

### Question 8
**In data storytelling, what is the ultimate goal?**

- To show as much raw data as possible.
- To let the audience interpret the data without guidance.
- To create the most aesthetically pleasing chart.
- **To move the audience from data to insight, and then to action.**

---

### Question 9
**What is "chartjunk"?**

- A chart that contains a forecast or projection.
- **Unnecessary visual elements that distract from the data.**
- A chart with a de-emphasized footnote.
- Any chart that uses a 100% stacked bar format.

---

### Question 10
**Before creating any visual, what is the most critical first step?**

- Choosing the color scheme for the chart.
- Gathering all available data on the topic.
- Deciding between a bar chart and a line chart.
- **Understanding the context, including the audience and goal.**

---

### Question 11
**In the model visual showing the "Expected director population," why are only the "Unmet need (gap)" values labeled?**

- **To keep the audience's focus on the growing leadership gap.**
- There was not enough space to label the other values.
- The other values were not important to the story.
- Because attrition values are always negative and do not need labels.

---

### Question 12
**According to the principles of visual hierarchy, how should color be used to guide attention?**

- Ensure the color palette is the same for every chart you create.
- Apply bright colors to all background elements.
- Use a different color for every category to distinguish them.
- **Use it sparingly to highlight the most important data.**

## Quiz Set 3: Advanced Visualizations

### Question 13
**What is a key characteristic of a steam chart?**

- It has a baseline x-axis to show positive and negative values.
- It is best used for comparing precise quantitative values.
- **It highlights the peaks, troughs, and "ebb and flow" of data.**
- It is designed to show a flow from a starting point to an end point.

---

### Question 14
**Which visualization is most appropriate for assessing associations and patterns in multivariate datasets, often for exploratory analysis?**

- Flow Map
- Stacked Area Chart
- Radial Network
- **Scatter Plot Matrix**

---

### Question 15
**What is a significant limitation of a choropleth map?**

- It is too complex for showing geographical data.
- It can only show data for a single point in time.
- It cannot use color to represent quantitative values.
- **The visual prominence of large geographic areas can distort the data's representation.**

---

### Question 16
**What is the main purpose of a network diagram (force-directed graph)?**

- To track the flow of a value from a start point to an end point.
- To show the exact geographical location of different nodes.
- To display a clear, uncluttered view of a simple dataset.
- **To explore complex relationships, clusters, and dominant nodes in a dataset.**

---

### Question 17
**Which chart type is designed to show the flow of a quantitative value as it is transformed across time and space, famously used to map Napoleon's march?**

- **Flow Map**
- Network Connection Map
- Stacked Area Chart
- Steam Chart

---

### Question 18
**What is a potential pitfall to be aware of when interpreting a radial network (or chord diagram)?**

- It cannot show the strength of connections between components.
- **The length of a connecting line can be misleading.**
- It is restricted by the limitations of an x and y axis.
- It is only suitable for showing hierarchical data.

- **They allow for rapid and effective comparisons across a series of similar small charts.**
- They represent the flow of data through different processes.
