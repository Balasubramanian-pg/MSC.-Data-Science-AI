# 35.2.3. Sampling Distribution of Aggregate Business Metrics

When transactional records are filtered or segmented by region, category, or state, we are isolating subsets of the population. The aggregate values calculated for these segments behave exactly like statistics drawn from a sampling distribution.

If we repeatedly slice our transactional database by different regions, the resulting metrics:

$$
\bar{x}_1, \bar{x}_2, \bar{x}_3, \dots
$$

form a distribution of segment performance. To evaluate these segments correctly, we must build calculated fields that accurately weight each transaction rather than relying on unweighted averages.

The standard error of these visual segments decreases as the volume of records:

$$
n
$$

increases, according to the standard relation:

$$
\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

where:
- 

$$
\sigma_{\bar{x}}
$$

= the standard error of the segment mean
- 

$$
\sigma
$$

= the population standard deviation of transactions
- 

$$
n
$$

= the transaction count of the segment

Larger datasets produce visually stable and reliable segments, whereas small segments are highly sensitive to individual outlying transactions.
