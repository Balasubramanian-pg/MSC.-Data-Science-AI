# 35.2.2. Why Point Estimates Are Fundamentally Incomplete

Suppose two analysts independently evaluate regional sales data for the same product line.
- Analyst A reports a regional average sales value of:

$$
\bar{x} = 450
$$

- Analyst B reports a regional average sales value of:

$$
\bar{x} = 480
$$

Neither analyst is incorrect. This variance occurs due to the subset of data each analyst selected, which illustrates the concept of sampling and segment variability. A single point estimate hides the variance within categories, the spatial distribution across states, and the negative performance of specific product segments.

The core insight of analytical dashboards is:

>[!Note]
> A visualization is a dynamic representation of an underlying multi-dimensional dataset.

Because of this, static summaries are insufficient. A dashboard must allow users to slice, filter, and drill down into the data to understand the underlying statistical distributions.
