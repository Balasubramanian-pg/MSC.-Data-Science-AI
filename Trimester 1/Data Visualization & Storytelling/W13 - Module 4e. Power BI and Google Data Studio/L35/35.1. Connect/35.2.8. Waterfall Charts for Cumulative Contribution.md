# 35.2.8. Waterfall Charts for Cumulative Contribution

While a tree map excels at showing total volume, a waterfall chart is ideal for showing how individual elements sequentially add to or subtract from a cumulative total. This is particularly useful for tracking how different product segments or subcategories contribute to the overall corporate profit.

In a horizontal waterfall chart:
- The vertical axis lists the categories or subcategories in descending order of their positive contribution.
- The horizontal axis measures the cumulative profit:

$$
\text{Profit} = \sum \text{Profit}
$$

- Positive contributions are colored green, negative contributions (representing losses) are colored red, and the final bar represents the total aggregated profit.

For example, if the Phone subcategory contributes highly to profit, its green bar extends the cumulative total. Conversely, if Tables operate at a net loss, its red bar shifts the cumulative total backward. This visualization allows executives to immediately identify which product lines are driving profits and which are eroding the bottom line.
