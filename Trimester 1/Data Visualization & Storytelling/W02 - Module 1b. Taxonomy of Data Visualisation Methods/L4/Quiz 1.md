## Question 1

**Correct Answer: When you want to focus attention on a single, important number.**

Eliminate the wrong answers:

* **When you need to compare multiple categories.** ❌ Bar charts or similar visualizations are better suited for comparisons.
* **When you need to show the relationship between many variables.** ❌ Scatter plots, bubble charts, or correlation matrices are more appropriate.
* **When you are presenting a large, complex dataset.** ❌ Simple text is ineffective for large and complex data.

**Remaining correct option:**

* **When you want to focus attention on a single, important number.** ✅

## Question 2

**Correct Answer: To use color saturation to guide the brain to points of interest.**

Eliminate the wrong answers:

* **To show the precise values in a large dataset.** ❌ Heatmaps emphasize patterns and intensity rather than exact values.
* **To interact with our verbal system so the audience reads the data.** ❌ This describes text-based displays, not heatmaps.
* **To display a trend over a continuous time series.** ❌ Line charts are typically used for time series trends.

**Remaining correct option:**

* **To use color saturation to guide the brain to points of interest.** ✅

## Question 3

**Correct Answer: Scatter plot**

Eliminate the wrong answers:

* **Waterfall chart** ❌ Used to show how sequential positive and negative values contribute to a total.
* **Line chart** ❌ Primarily used to show trends over time or ordered sequences.
* **Horizontal bar chart** ❌ Best for comparing categories, not relationships between quantitative variables.

**Remaining correct option:**

* **Scatter plot** ✅

## Question 4

**Correct Answer: Make the table data-centric so the data stands out.**

Eliminate the wrong answers:

* **Ensure the audience must read the table to understand it.** ❌ Tables should be designed for quick comprehension, not force extensive reading.
* **Use different colors for every other row to improve readability.** ❌ Alternating row colors may help readability but are not the key design principle.
* **Use heavy borders to clearly separate all cells.** ❌ Heavy borders create visual clutter and distract from the data.

**Remaining correct option:**

* **Make the table data-centric so the data stands out.** ✅
