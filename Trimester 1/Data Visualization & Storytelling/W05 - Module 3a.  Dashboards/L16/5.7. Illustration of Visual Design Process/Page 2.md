Icons and images draw the attention and provide additional context for the audience 
to decipher the data. Then you have drawing objects which are geometrical shapes 
that are used within the data dashboard to indicate relationship between graphics or 
within the graph. So, in the experiential learning session, we will see the usage of each 
of the six categories to enhance the expressiveness and effectiveness of the visual 
and text we have already discussed. 
  
Text is one of the best ways to highlight and portray information that you want to 
convey to the audience. In the dashboard design, the pop-up box which is nothing but 
a textual element coming on the graphic. For example, the earlier dashboard when 
you select on Belgium, this is a pop-up box, nothing but a textual element containing 
four pieces of information saying that this is country Belgium indicating the woman 
legend, the orange dot saying woman and 25.9 being the ages years of life expectancy 
for women and this is captured in a simple text which is coming out as a pop-up. 
  
So, these elements can enhance the appeal of your visual and most importantly can 
bring in the insight that you want to drive to the audience in a more effective manner. 
And then you have organisers especially the small multiples, tables and spatial maps 
which will help you to better present your data which is otherwise widespread. Let us 
take the example of GDP growth rates across the country. 
  
It could be presented on a spatial map giving a feeling of how growth rates compare 
with other countries very intuitively. The same could not be achieved by using a bar 
graph or a table. You can use small multiples depending upon the use case where 
there are multiple categories and the same phenomenon has to be observed across 
all of them. 
  
In these cases, small multiples might be very useful. So, depending upon the purpose 
at hand, you might be using design visuals to enhance the expressiveness and 
effectiveness of your dashboard.
