Or you can have a neutral area which is neither emphasised or de-emphasised by

And in fact, you can create much more particular emphasis by creating a border 
around the graphic and highlighting it in white colour, visually drawing attention of the 
audience to that particular region. 
  
These are different types of uses of contrast and colour to bring emphasis on a sub-
regions of the dashboard where you want to draw attention or you want to de-
emphasise that region so that the attention is not spent on that particular area. Typical 
rule of thumb or guiding principles for bringing more emphasis on to the dashboard, 
always use the top left centre areas of the dashboard to receive the greatest emphasis. 
Top left and centre areas of the dashboard should receive the greatest emphasis with 
key information going into those areas. 
  
Because if you remember cognitive abilities of humans and types of memory, we are 
attuned to read the information from top left to right and top to bottom when we look 
at any information. It is the preconditioning of the human brain that makes you to read 
any information from top left to right and from top to bottom. So by arranging the key 
elements that you want to highlight on your dashboard at the top left corner, you are 
bringing natural attention of your audience to that particular region. 
  
Similarly, you can also draw central attention to a central region by creating a border 
around it. So central regions are also a very attentive spaces within the dashboard, 
but you need to create a border around it to bring much more emphasis. And avoid 
placing non-essential items like logos and navigational controls in the prime areas 
because this is where the natural attention would go.
