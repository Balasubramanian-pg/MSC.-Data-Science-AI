Look, you have to understand: this isn't just a random collection of [material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material). It’s something far more serious than that, related to [information architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_architecture).

What you’re looking at here is a carefully structured [repository](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repository) - a [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map) of [meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaning_(philosophy)), if you will - comprising the foundational [text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text) and [reference books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_work) that guided my path. And more than that, it contains the comprehensive [document](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Document)-the [PDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDF)-that I meticulously brought into being through the sheer, disciplined act of [voluntary attention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention) while wrestling with these [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept).
It’s an attempt to extract the [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal) from the [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise), and to establish a stable, functional [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture) of [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge) in the face of absolute [chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_theory). And that's no simple thing.
