# 8.1.7. Conclusions

Data distribution is the statistical fingerprint of a dataset. Understanding its shape determines how the entire machine learning pipeline should behave, from feature scaling to model selection and statistical validation.

### 7.1 Summary of Distribution Metrics

The following table summarizes the primary metrics used to explain a dataset's distribution and their sensitivity to outliers.

| Metric Category | Specific Metric | Formula / Definition | Sensitivity to Outliers |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Central Tendency | Mean | $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i}{n}$$ | Highly Sensitive |
| Central Tendency | Median | Middle value of sorted data | Robust |
| Spread | Standard Deviation | $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \mu)^2}{n}}$$ | Highly Sensitive |
| Spread | Interquartile Range | $$Q_3 - Q_1$$ | Robust |

### 7.2 Final Takeaways

The flow of data explainability moves from raw distribution to central tendency, then to spread and skewness, ultimately dictating model choice and statistical validity.

>[!Tip]
> Always compute both the mean and the median before modeling. If they differ significantly, your data is skewed, and you must apply appropriate transformations or choose robust algorithms to prevent your model from being dragged toward the outliers.
