# 8.1.2. Central Tendency Measures

Transitioning from the general concept of distribution to specific metrics, we first examine central tendency. These measures identify the center or typical value of a dataset, providing a single summary statistic that represents the bulk of the data.

The three major measures of central tendency are summarized in the following table.

| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean | Arithmetic average |
| Median | Middle value after sorting |
| Mode | Most frequently occurring value |

### 2.1 The Mean

The mean is the arithmetic average of all observations. It is the most common measure of central tendency for continuous, symmetric data.

The formula for the sample mean is:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i}{n}
$$

where:
- $$\bar{x}$$ = sample mean
- $$x_i$$ = individual sample observations
- $$n$$ = sample size

To emphasize the sample mean formula, we restate it:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i}{n}
$$

### What should you be aware of ?
- The most critical property of the mean is its extreme sensitivity to outliers. 
- Because every observation contributes directly to the sum, a single extreme value can pull the mean far away from the center of the bulk of the data. 
- This makes the mean unstable and potentially misleading in skewed distributions.

### 2.2 The Median

The median is the middle value of a dataset after it has been sorted in ascending order. 

- The key requirement for calculating the median is that the data must be sorted first.
- If the dataset contains an odd number of observations, the median is the exact middle value.
- If the dataset contains an even number of observations, the median is computed as the average of the two middle values.

The most important property of the median is its **robustness to outliers**. 
- Because it depends only on the rank order of the values and not their magnitude, extreme values have virtually no effect on the median. 
- This is why the median is strictly preferred for skewed distributions, such as household income or property prices.

### 2.3 The Mode

The mode is the most frequently occurring value in a dataset. 

A dataset can have one mode (**unimodal**), two modes (**bimodal**), or multiple modes (**multimodal**). 
>[!Tip]
>A bimodal distribution often indicates the presence of two distinct subpopulations within the data, such as the heights of a mixed group of adults and children.
