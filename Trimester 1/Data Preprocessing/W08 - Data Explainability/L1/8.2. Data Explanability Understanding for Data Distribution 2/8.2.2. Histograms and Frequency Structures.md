# 8.2.2. Histograms and Frequency Structures

A histogram is one of the most important visualization tools in statistics. It groups continuous values into intervals called bins and counts how many observations fall inside each bin.

The structure of a histogram is defined by two axes:
- **X-axis**: Represents the value ranges or bins.
- **Y-axis**: Represents the frequency or count of observations in each bin.

Higher bars indicate that more data is concentrated in that specific range. Histograms reveal where data clusters, whether the data is symmetric, the presence of skewness, potential outliers, and the overall shape of the distribution.

>[!Note]
> The distribution shape becomes visually obvious in a histogram, allowing us to immediately assess whether the data clusters around a central value or spreads out evenly.
