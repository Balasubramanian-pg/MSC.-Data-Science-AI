# 8.2.7. Bimodal and Complex Distributions

<img width="782" height="687" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a47c553c-edf8-45e2-82c0-6e7a017e3422" />

Beyond the standard normal and uniform shapes, real-world data often exhibits complex structures. 
>[!Note]
>A **bimodal distribution** contains two distinct concentration peaks.

This shape often suggests the presence of two distinct subpopulations or mixed generating processes within the dataset. For example, the distribution of heights in a room containing both adults and children will be bimodal. Recognizing bimodality is extremely important in clustering and mixture models, as it indicates hidden grouping structures that must be modeled separately.
