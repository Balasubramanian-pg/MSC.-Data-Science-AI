# 8.2.6. The Uniform Distribution

In stark contrast to the normal distribution, the uniform distribution is defined by the property that every value within a specified range has an equal probability of occurring.

Its shape is a flat rectangle, exhibiting no peaks and no central concentration. The probability structure is completely flat, meaning the likelihood of observing any specific value is equal.

An example of a uniform distribution is the distribution of birth dates across a year. The probability of being born on January 1st is roughly equal to the probability of being born on December 10th, resulting in an evenly distributed frequency.

The following table compares the normal and uniform distributions.

| Feature | Normal Distribution | Uniform Distribution |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Shape | Bell curve | Rectangle |
| Concentration | Around the center | Equal everywhere |
| Mean/Median/Mode | Approximately equal | Often flat or undefined |
| Probability Structure | Central clustering | Equal likelihood |
