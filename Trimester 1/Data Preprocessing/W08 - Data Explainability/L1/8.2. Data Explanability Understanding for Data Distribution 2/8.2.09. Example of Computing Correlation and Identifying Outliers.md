# 8.2.9. Example of Computing Correlation and Identifying Outliers

To solidify the mathematical concepts of correlation and outlier detection, we apply the formulas to a concrete scenario.

Suppose:
- Dataset X (Age in years): 20, 25, 30, 35, 40
- Dataset Y (Salary in thousands): 30, 35, 45, 50, 60
- We want to compute the Pearson correlation coefficient and identify if there is a positive, negative, or no correlation.

### Step 1: Compute the Means of X and Y

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{20 + 25 + 30 + 35 + 40}{5} = \frac{150}{5} = 30
$$

$$
\bar{y} = \frac{30 + 35 + 45 + 50 + 60}{5} = \frac{220}{5} = 44
$$

### Step 2: Calculate Deviations from the Mean

For X: $$-10, -5, 0, 5, 10$$
For Y: $$-14, -9, 1, 6, 16$$

### Step 3: Compute the Covariance Numerator

Multiply the deviations and sum them:

$$
(-10)(-14) + (-5)(-9) + (0)(1) + (5)(6) + (10)(16)
$$

$$
= 140 + 45 + 0 + 30 + 160 = 375
$$

### Step 4: Compute the Sum of Squared Deviations

For X: $$(-10)^2 + (-5)^2 + 0^2 + 5^2 + 10^2 = 100 + 25 + 0 + 25 + 100 = 250$$

For Y: $$(-14)^2 + (-9)^2 + 1^2 + 6^2 + 16^2 = 196 + 81 + 1 + 36 + 256 = 570$$

### Step 5: Compute the Correlation Coefficient

$$
r = \frac{375}{\sqrt{250 \times 570}} = \frac{375}{\sqrt{142500}} = \frac{375}{377.49} \approx 0.993
$$

The correlation coefficient is **0.993**, indicating a very strong **positive correlation** between age and salary in this dataset.
