# 8.2.8. Common Failure Modes in Distribution Analysis

Ignoring the visual and statistical structure of data leads to predictable and severe modeling failures. We must avoid the following common traps.

**Using the Mean on Highly Skewed Data**: This produces misleading summaries. The mean will be dragged toward the tail, failing to represent the typical observation.

**Ignoring Outliers**: Failing to identify and handle outliers can severely distort the mean, inflate the variance, and destabilize model training.

**Assuming Normality Everywhere**: Many real-world datasets are not Gaussian. Applying parametric tests that assume normality to heavily skewed data yields invalid p-values and confidence intervals.

**Misinterpreting Correlation**: Assuming that a high correlation coefficient implies a causal relationship is a fundamental logical error.

>[!Tip]
> Always visualize your data before modeling. A simple histogram or box plot can reveal skewness, outliers, and multimodality that numerical summaries completely hide.
