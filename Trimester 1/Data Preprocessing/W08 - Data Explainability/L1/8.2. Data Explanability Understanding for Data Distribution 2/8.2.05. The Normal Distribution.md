# 8.2.5. The Normal Distribution

<img width="1024" height="640" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8313333e-6e05-4798-992e-8258013a6982" />

The normal distribution, also known as the **Gaussian distribution** or **bell curve**, is the most important continuous probability distribution in statistics. 

Its shape is perfectly symmetric and bell-shaped, with the highest concentration of data near the center. In a normal distribution, the measures of central tendency are equal:

$$\text{Mean} \approx \text{Median} \approx \text{Mode}$$

To emphasize this defining property, we restate it:

$$\text{Mean} \approx \text{Median} \approx \text{Mode}$$

The normal distribution is defined by two parameters:
- **Mean ($$\mu$$)**: Determines the center location of the distribution.
- **Standard Deviation ($$\sigma$$)**: Determines the width or spread of the distribution.

Most data lies near the mean, with approximately 95% to 98% of observations lying within two standard deviations of the center. Real-world examples of normally distributed data include human height, IQ scores, and blood pressure, where extreme values become increasingly rare.
