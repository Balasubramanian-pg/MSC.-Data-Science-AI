# 8.3.5. LIME: Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations

The first major local explainability method is LIME. It is one of the most widely used techniques for interpreting black box models.

### 5.1 Core Idea Behind LIME

Suppose the real model is extremely complex, featuring nonlinear decision boundaries or deep learning architectures. Understanding feature importance globally becomes computationally and cognitively difficult. 

The central insight of LIME is to approximate the complex model locally with a simple, interpretable model. Globally, the decision boundary may be highly irregular, but locally, it can be approximated by a simple linear model.

### 5.2 How LIME Works

The LIME workflow follows a strict sequence of operations.

**Step 1: Select a Sample**. Choose the specific data point to explain, such as one loan application or one customer profile.

**Step 2: Generate Perturbed Samples**. Create many artificial samples near the original sample by adding small deltas to the features. These are called perturbations.

**Step 3: Query the Original Black Box Model**. Pass all perturbed samples into the original complex model and obtain predictions for each.

**Step 4: Fit a Simple Local Model**. Train a simple interpretable model, usually a linear model, near the selected point. This local model approximates the complex model in a small neighborhood.

**Step 5: Interpret Feature Weights**. The weights of the local linear model indicate which features most influenced the prediction for that specific instance.

>[!Note]
> LIME does not explain the entire model. It explains one prediction at a time. This localized approach is its greatest strength and its primary limitation.
