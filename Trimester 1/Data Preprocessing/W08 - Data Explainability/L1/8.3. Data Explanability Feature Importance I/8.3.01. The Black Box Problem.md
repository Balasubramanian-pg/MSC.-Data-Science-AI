# 8.3.1. The Black Box Problem

Modern machine learning systems are often highly accurate, highly complex, and poorly interpretable. 

- This is especially true for deep neural networks, ensemble methods, and complex nonlinear classifiers.
- These models can make excellent predictions but often cannot explain why a prediction was made. This phenomenon is known as black box modeling.

### 1.1 Core Pipeline Context

- The standard data science pipeline flows from data to preprocessing, to a machine learning model, and finally to prediction or knowledge. 
- The critical issue arises at the final stage: predictions are generated, but the reasoning behind them remains entirely unclear.

### 1.2 Why Explainability Matters

Prediction alone is insufficient in high-stakes systems. Suppose an artificial intelligence model predicts a medical image is cancerous. That alone is not enough. Doctors also need to know why it was classified as cancer, which image regions mattered, and which features influenced the decision. Without explanation, trust, validation, and accountability become exceedingly difficult to achieve.
