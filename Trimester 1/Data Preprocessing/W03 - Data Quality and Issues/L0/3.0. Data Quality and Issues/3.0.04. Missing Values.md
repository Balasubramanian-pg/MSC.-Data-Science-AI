# 3.0.4. Missing Values

Transitioning from extreme values to absent values, we encounter missing data. Missing values occur when no value is stored for an attribute in a particular record. This is one of the most common and challenging data quality issues in real-world datasets.

### 3.0.4.1. Mechanisms of Missingness

Understanding why data is missing is crucial for selecting the correct handling technique. Statisticians classify missing data into three mechanisms.

**Missing Completely at Random (MCAR)**: The probability of a value being missing is entirely unrelated to any observed or unobserved data. The missingness is purely random.

**Missing at Random (MAR)**: The probability of a value being missing is related to the observed data, but not to the missing value itself. For example, younger users might be less likely to report their income, but the missingness depends only on age (which is observed), not on the income itself.

**Missing Not at Random (MNAR)**: The probability of a value being missing is related to the unobserved missing value itself. For example, people with very high incomes might be more likely to refuse to report their income. 

>[!Warning]
> MNAR is the most dangerous mechanism. If the missingness depends on the unobserved value, simply imputing the mean will introduce severe bias into your dataset.
