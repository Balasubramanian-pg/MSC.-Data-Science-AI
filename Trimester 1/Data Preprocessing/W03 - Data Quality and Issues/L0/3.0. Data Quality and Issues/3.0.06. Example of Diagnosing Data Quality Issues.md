# 3.0.6. Example of Diagnosing Data Quality Issues

To solidify the concepts of data quality, we apply diagnostic reasoning to a concrete scenario.

Suppose:
- A dataset of customer ages: 25, 28, 22, -5, 30, 29, 27, 200, 26, 24
- We need to identify and classify the data quality issues.

### Step 1: Scan for Logical Inconsistencies
Reviewing the values, we observe an age of -5. Age cannot be negative. This is a logical inconsistency and a clear data entry error.

### Step 2: Scan for Outliers
We observe an age of 200. While theoretically possible in a biological sense, in a standard customer dataset, this is an extreme deviation. This is an outlier, likely a data entry error (e.g., typing 200 instead of 20).

### Step 3: Assess the Impact on the Mean
If we calculate the mean including the errors:
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{25 + 28 + 22 - 5 + 30 + 29 + 27 + 200 + 26 + 24}{10} = \frac{406}{10} = 40.6
$$

### Step 4: Assess the Impact After Correction
If we remove or correct the invalid values (-5 and 200), the sum of the remaining 8 valid values is 211.
$$
\bar{x}_{\text{corrected}} = \frac{211}{8} = 26.375
$$

### Step 5: Final Diagnosis
The original mean of **40.6** is highly distorted by the outlier and inconsistency. The corrected mean of **26.375** accurately reflects the central tendency of the valid data. This demonstrates how a single poor-quality record can fundamentally alter statistical conclusions.
