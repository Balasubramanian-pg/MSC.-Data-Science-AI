# 10.6.5. Example of Computing Euclidean Distance

Suppose:
- An analyst is comparing two regional pharmaceutical markets based strictly on two standardized numerical metrics: Monthly Revenue and Total Sales Representatives.
- The two-dimensional coordinates for Market A are:
  $$p_1 = 10$$
  and
  $$p_2 = 5$$
- The two-dimensional coordinates for Market B are:
  $$q_1 = 6$$
  and
  $$q_2 = 2$$

### Step 1: State the Distance Formula
$$d(p, q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{2} (p_i - q_i)^2}$$

### Step 2: Extract the Given Coordinates
$$p_1 = 10$$
and
$$p_2 = 5$$
and
$$q_1 = 6$$
and
$$q_2 = 2$$

### Step 3: Compute the Squared Differences for Each Dimension
$$(10 - 6)^2 = 16$$
and
$$(5 - 2)^2 = 9$$

### Step 4: Sum the Squared Differences
$$16 + 9 = 25$$

### Step 5: Calculate the Final Distance
**$$d(p, q) = 5$$**
