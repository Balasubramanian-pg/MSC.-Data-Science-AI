# 10.6.15. Example of a K-Means Centroid Update

Suppose:
- An algorithm is updating a specific centroid for a newly formed cluster containing exactly three one-dimensional data points.
- The first assigned standardized data point is:
  $$x_1 = 4$$
- The second assigned standardized data point is:
  $$x_2 = 8$$
- The third assigned standardized data point is:
  $$x_3 = 6$$

### Step 1: Identify the Centroid Update Formula
$$\mu_{new} = \frac{\sum x_i}{N}$$

### Step 2: Extract the Assigned Data Points
$$x_1 = 4$$
and
$$x_2 = 8$$
and
$$x_3 = 6$$

### Step 3: Compute the Sum of the Coordinates
$$4 + 8 + 6 = 18$$

### Step 4: Identify the Total Number of Points
$$N = 3$$

### Step 5: Calculate the New Centroid Coordinate
**$$\mu_{new} = 6$$**
(The geometric centroid physically moves its mathematical position to coordinate 6 for the next iterative assignment phase).
