# 2.4.8. Conclusions

Sampling theory bridges the gap between observed data and population truth. The validity of any statistical conclusion rests entirely on the representativeness of the sample and the correct calculation of standard errors.

The following table summarizes the primary sampling techniques and their key characteristics.

### Completed Table: Sampling Techniques

| Sampling Method | Selection Mechanism | Key Advantage | Primary Risk |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Simple Random** | Equal probability (random draw) | Unbiased, simple theory | May miss small subgroups |
| **Stratified** | Random within strata | Ensures subgroup representation | Requires prior population info |
| **Cluster** | Random clusters | Cost-effective for geography | Higher sampling error |
| **Systematic** | Every *k*-th individual from random start | Easy to implement, less random error than simple random | Periodic patterns in list can bias results |
| **Convenience** | Non-random (easiest to reach) | Fast, cheap | Severe selection bias |
| **Purposive** | Non-random (researcher's judgment) | Targets specific useful participants | High subjective bias |
| **Quota** | Non-random (fill predetermined proportions) | Ensures demographic balance | Selection within quotas remains biased |
| **Snowball** | Non-random (referrals from existing subjects) | Access to hard-to-reach populations | Network bias (friends share traits) |
