# 15. Interview-Style Insights

**Interviewer:** "What is the difference between Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation?"
**Candidate:** "MLE finds the parameter that maximizes the likelihood of the data $P(X|\theta)$. MAP finds the parameter that maximizes the posterior probability $P(\theta|X)$. By Bayes' theorem, MAP incorporates a prior distribution $P(\theta)$. Therefore, MAP is equivalent to MLE with an added regularization term derived from the prior. For example, L2 regularization (Ridge) is equivalent to MAP with a Gaussian prior, and L1 regularization (Lasso) is equivalent to MAP with a Laplace prior."

**Interviewer:** "What are the asymptotic properties of the MLE?"
**Candidate:** "Under regularity conditions, as the sample size $n$ approaches infinity, the MLE is consistent (it converges in probability to the true parameter), asymptotically normal (its distribution approaches a Gaussian centered at the true parameter), and efficient (it achieves the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound, meaning it has the lowest possible variance among all unbiased estimators)."
