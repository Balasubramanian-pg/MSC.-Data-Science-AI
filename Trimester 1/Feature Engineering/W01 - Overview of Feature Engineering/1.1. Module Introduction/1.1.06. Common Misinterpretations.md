# 1.1.6. Common Misinterpretations

Many who approach this craft fall into predictable intellectual traps. We must guard against them.

### Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> "A more complex algorithm can compensate for poorly engineered features."

Wrong. 
Complexity without quality is merely overfitting in disguise. A simple linear model on excellent features will consistently outperform a deep neural network on garbage data.

### Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> "Feature engineering is a one-time task completed before modeling begins."

Wrong.
It is an iterative dialogue with the data. As models fail, they reveal the inadequacies of your features, demanding that you return to the forge and craft better representations.

### Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> "More features always yield better predictive power."

Not necessarily.
Beyond a certain threshold, adding features introduces the curse of dimensionality. The space becomes so sparse that distance loses its meaning, and the model begins to memorize noise rather than learning signal.
