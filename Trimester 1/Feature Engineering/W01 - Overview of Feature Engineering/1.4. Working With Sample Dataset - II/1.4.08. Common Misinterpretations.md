# 1.4.8. Common Misinterpretations

Many practitioners fall into predictable traps when engineering features for chemical datasets, leading to degraded model performance.

### 8.1 Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> **"High correlation with the target guarantees a feature is useful."**

Wrong.

Correlation only measures linear relationships. A feature might have zero linear correlation but possess a highly nonlinear relationship with the target that a tree-based model could still exploit.

### 8.2 Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> **"Random Forests do not require feature scaling."**

Wrong under pipeline optimization.

While the splitting logic of a single tree is invariant to monotonic scaling, standardizing the data is a pragmatic necessity when the pipeline includes distance-based models, neural networks, or regularization techniques.

### 8.3 Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> **"Features with low importance should always be deleted immediately."**

Not necessarily.

A feature might possess low global importance but act as a crucial interaction term when combined with another variable. Blindly deleting features before testing interaction terms can destroy edge-case accuracy.
