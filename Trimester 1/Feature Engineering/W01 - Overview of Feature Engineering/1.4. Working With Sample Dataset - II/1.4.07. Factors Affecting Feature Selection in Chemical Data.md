# 1.4.7. Factors Affecting Feature Selection in Chemical Data

### 7.1 Multicollinearity
Chemical properties in wine are often highly interdependent, such as total sulfur dioxide and free sulfur dioxide.
Highly correlated features inflate model variance and destabilize coefficient estimates in linear models.

### 7.2 Signal-to-Noise Ratio
Features with low correlation to the target introduce variance without adding predictive power.
Thus, removing them leads to higher model generalization and faster convergence.

### 7.3 Scale Discrepancy
Chemical measurements exist on vastly different scales, from trace minerals to bulk alcohol percentages.
Failing to standardize these features can cause distance-based algorithms to completely ignore the trace elements.

The following table summarizes how these factors impact overall model performance.

| Factor | Impact on Variance | Impact on Convergence |
| :--- | :---: | ---: |
| Multicollinearity | Very High | Unstable |
| Signal-to-Noise Ratio | High | Slow |
| Scale Discrepancy | Medium | Very Slow |

Avoiding these pitfalls ensures your feature space remains mathematically sound.
