# 1.3.8. Common Misinterpretations

Many who approach this craft fall into predictable intellectual traps. We must guard against them.

### Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> **"A feature with low importance is entirely useless and must be deleted."**
Wrong.
A feature might possess low global importance but act as a crucial tie-breaker in a specific, rare subset of the data. Discarding it blindly may destroy edge-case accuracy.

### Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> **"Visual separation in a violin plot guarantees high algorithmic importance."**
Wrong under complex ensemble methods.
While visual separation indicates strong univariate predictive power, a Random Forest evaluates features in the context of multivariate interactions. A visually overlapping feature might still be highly important when combined with another variable.

### Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> **"We should always use all available features to maximize information."**
Not necessarily.
Beyond a certain threshold, adding features with low discriminative power introduces the curse of dimensionality. The model begins to memorize noise rather than learning the underlying signal, degrading its ability to generalize to unseen data.

Avoiding these pitfalls ensures your analytical conclusions remain robust and grounded in reality.
