# 1.1.4. The Challenges of High-Dimensional Data

As datasets grow in complexity, the feature space can become overwhelmed by irrelevant variables. This phenomenon introduces severe mathematical and computational challenges.

In high-dimensional spaces, the curse of dimensionality causes distance metrics to lose their discriminative power. As the number of dimensions $$d$$ approaches infinity, the relative difference between the nearest and farthest data points converges to zero.

$$
\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{D_{\max} - D_{\min}}{D_{\min}} = 0
$$

where:

- $$d$$ = the number of dimensions in the feature space

- $$D_{\max}$$ = the distance to the farthest point

- $$D_{\min}$$ = the distance to the nearest point

>[!Warning]
> **High-dimensional data significantly increases the risk of overfitting.** When the feature space is too sparse, models memorize noise instead of learning generalizable patterns, elevating computational costs and obscuring interpretability.

To manage this complexity, we must deploy a structured toolkit of engineering techniques.
