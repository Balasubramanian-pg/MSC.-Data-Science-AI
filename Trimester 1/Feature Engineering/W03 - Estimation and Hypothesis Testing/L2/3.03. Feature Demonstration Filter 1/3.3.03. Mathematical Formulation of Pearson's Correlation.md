# 3.3.3. Mathematical Formulation of Pearson's Correlation

The correlation coefficient is derived by taking the covariance of two variables and normalizing it. 

The core formula for Pearson's correlation is:

$$
r_{xy} = \frac{\text{cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_x \sigma_y}
$$

Where:

- $$r_{xy}$$ = Pearson's correlation coefficient
    
- $$\text{cov}(X, Y)$$ = covariance between variables $$X$$ and $$Y$$
    
- $$\sigma_x$$ = standard deviation of variable $$X$$
    
- $$\sigma_y$$ = standard deviation of variable $$Y$$
    

By dividing the covariance by the product of the individual standard deviations, the resulting metric becomes dimensionless. Normalization ensures that the scale of the original variables does not distort the measurement of their relationship.

Understanding this mathematical foundation allows us to interpret the resulting values in a standardized way.
