# 3.3.2. Theory of Pearson's Correlation

When dealing with continuous numerical variables, **Pearson's correlation coefficient** serves as the primary filter metric. 

This coefficient measures the strength and the direction of the linear relationship between two continuous variables. By capturing how two variables change together linearly, Pearson's correlation allows data scientists to quantify the relevance of a feature to the prediction task. 

The metric strictly evaluates linear proportionality. If an increase in one variable consistently corresponds to a proportional increase in another, the correlation is highly positive. Conversely, if an increase corresponds to a proportional decrease, the correlation is highly negative.
