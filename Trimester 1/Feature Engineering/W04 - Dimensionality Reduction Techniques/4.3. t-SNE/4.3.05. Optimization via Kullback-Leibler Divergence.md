# 4.3.5. Optimization via Kullback-Leibler Divergence

The ultimate goal of the algorithm is to arrange the low-dimensional points so that the probability distribution matches the high-dimensional distribution as closely as possible.

This is achieved by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence:

$$
KL(P || Q) = \sum_{i \neq j} p_{ij} \log \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}
$$

where:

- $$P$$ = high-dimensional probability distribution
- $$Q$$ = low-dimensional probability distribution
- $$p_{ij}$$ = original affinities
- $$q_{ij}$$ = projected affinities

By repeatedly applying gradient descent to this cost function, t-SNE updates the coordinates in the low-dimensional space until a stable local minimum is reached.

For maximum emphasis on the cost function driving the entire algorithm, we restate the optimization objective:

$$
KL(P || Q) = \sum_{i \neq j} p_{ij} \log \frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}
$$
