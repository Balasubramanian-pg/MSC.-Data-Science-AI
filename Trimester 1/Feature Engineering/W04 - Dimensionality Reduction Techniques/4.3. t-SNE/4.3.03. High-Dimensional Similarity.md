# 4.3.3. High-Dimensional Similarity

For every target data point, we compute the similarity to all other points using a Gaussian distribution centered on that target point.

The conditional probability of point:

$$
x_j
$$

being chosen as a neighbor by point:

$$
x_i
$$

is defined as:

$$
p_{j|i} = \frac{\exp\left(- \frac{\|x_i - x_j\|^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp\left(- \frac{\|x_i - x_k\|^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)}
$$

where:

- $$x_i$$ = original target data point
- $$x_j$$ = potential neighbor data point
- $$\sigma_i$$= variance of the Gaussian distribution centered at$$x_i$$
- $$\|x_i - x_j\|^2$$ = squared Euclidean distance between the points

Because we want a symmetric probability matrix for optimization, the final high-dimensional similarity is mathematically averaged:

$$
p_{ij} = \frac{p_{j|i} + p_{i|j}}{2N}
$$

where:

- $$N$$ = total number of data points
- $$p_{ij}$$ = symmetric high-dimensional probability
