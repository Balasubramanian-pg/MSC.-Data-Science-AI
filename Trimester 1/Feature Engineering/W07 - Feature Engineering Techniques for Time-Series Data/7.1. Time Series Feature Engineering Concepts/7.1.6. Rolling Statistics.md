# 7.1.6. Rolling Statistics

Rolling statistics summarize local behavior within a moving window.

### 6.1 Rolling Mean

The rolling mean at time $$t$$with window size$$w$$ is:

$$
\bar{Y}_t^{(w)} = \frac{1}{w} \sum_{i=0}^{w-1} Y_{t-i}
$$

where:

- $$w$$ = window size (number of periods)

- $$Y_{t-i}$$= value at time$$t-i$$

The rolling mean smooths noise to reveal underlying local trends. Larger $$w$$ produces smoother series but lags more behind sudden changes.

### 6.2 Rolling Standard Deviation

The rolling standard deviation quantifies local volatility:

$$
\sigma_t^{(w)} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{w-1} \sum_{i=0}^{w-1} \left(Y_{t-i} - \bar{Y}_t^{(w)}\right)^2}
$$

where:

- $$\sigma_t^{(w)}$ = rolling standard deviation at time $$t$$

- $$\bar{Y}_t^{(w)}$$= rolling mean at time$$t$$

High rolling standard deviation indicates periods of instability; low values indicate stability.

### 6.3 Rolling Trend (Slope) Features

Fitting a linear regression within a moving window captures the local rate of change. For window $$w$$, we estimate:

$$
Y_{t-i} = \beta_{0t} + \beta_{1t}(t-i) + \epsilon_{t-i}, \quad i = 0, 1, \dots, w-1
$$

The slope coefficient $$\beta_{1t}$$represents the local trend direction at time$$t$$:

- $$\beta_{1t} > 0$$ indicates an upward trend

- $$\beta_{1t} < 0$$ indicates a downward trend

- $$\beta_{1t} \approx 0$$ indicates a flat or stable period

This is more informative than the rolling mean alone because it distinguishes between levels and directions of change.
