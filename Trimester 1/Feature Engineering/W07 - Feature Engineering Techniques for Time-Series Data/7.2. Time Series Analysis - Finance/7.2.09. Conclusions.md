# 7.2.9. Conclusions

Domain-informed feature engineering transforms raw OHLCV data into structured indicators that capture market psychology, momentum, and volatility.

### 9.1 Anatomy of Financial Feature Engineering

The structure of every financial feature set follows:

$$
\text{Feature Vector}_t = \left[ \text{Lags}_t,\ \text{Rolling}_t,\ \text{Momentum}_t,\ \text{Volatility}_t \right]
$$

- **Lag features**: Encode recent price memory

- **Rolling features**: Summarize local trend and volatility

- **Momentum features** (RSI): Capture speed and magnitude of price changes

- **Volatility features** (Bollinger Bands): Define dynamic ranges of normal behavior

### 9.2 Indicator Comparison

| Indicator | Type | Primary Signal | Key Parameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moving Average | Trend | Local fair value | Window size $$w$$ |
| RSI | Momentum | Overbought/oversold | Window size $$w$$ |
| Bollinger Bands | Volatility | Normal price range | Window size $$w$$ |

### 9.3 Critical Principles

Understanding the temporal and statistical nature of financial data is essential:

- **Leakage destroys validity**: Features must use only past data relative to the prediction point

- **Stationarity matters**: Non-stationary series require transformation before modeling

- **Parsimony protects**: Correlated indicators waste capacity and increase variance

>[!Tip]
> "The best financial models combine statistical rigor with market intuition. Moving averages reveal what prices have done; RSI reveals what momentum implies; Bollinger Bands reveal what volatility permits."

You are now equipped with the technical foundation to engineer domain-specific features from financial time series data. The next step is implementing rigorous time-series cross-validation to ensure your indicators generalize to future, unseen market conditions.
