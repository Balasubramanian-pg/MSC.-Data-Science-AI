# 7.2.2. Why Raw Price Data Is Fundamentally Incomplete

Suppose two traders analyze the same stock.

- Trader A uses only today's closing price to predict tomorrow's movement

- Trader B uses a 14-day moving average, recent volatility, and momentum indicators

Trader B's model will almost certainly outperform Trader A's.

This happens because financial prices exhibit **temporal dependency**, **volatility clustering**, and **mean-reverting tendencies**. The key insight is:

>[!Note]
> A stock price is not an independent random draw. It is a realization of a stochastic process where past behavior conditions future distributions.

That means the price at time $$t$$ has its own probability structure conditional on history. The past directly influences the present through investor memory, market sentiment, and autocorrelated order flow.
