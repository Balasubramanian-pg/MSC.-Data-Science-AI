# 7.2.1. From Raw Prices to Market Intelligence

Financial markets generate vast quantities of raw data.

Every trading day produces an OHLCV record: Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume. A single stock observed over one year yields approximately 252 rows of these five columns. In isolation, each number describes a moment. Collectively, they describe a stochastic process evolving through time.

The raw close price at time $$t$$ is:

$$
P_t
$$

where:

- $$P_t$$ = closing price at time $$t$$

- $$t = 1, 2, \dots, T$$

This raw price is a **point estimate** of value at a single instant. It contains no memory, no context, and no measure of uncertainty. A model trained on raw prices alone would miss the temporal structure that defines financial markets.

The solution is **domain-informed feature engineering**: transforming raw OHLCV data into indicators that capture momentum, volatility, and trend direction.
