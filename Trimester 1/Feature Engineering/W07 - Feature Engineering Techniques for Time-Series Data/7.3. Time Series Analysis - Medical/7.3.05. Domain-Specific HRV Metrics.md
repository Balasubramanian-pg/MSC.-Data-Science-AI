# 7.3.5. Domain-Specific HRV Metrics

### 5.1 SDNN: Standard Deviation of RR Intervals

SDNN is the most common global measure of HRV, computed over a recording period:

$$
\text{SDNN} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} \left(\text{RR}_i - \overline{\text{RR}}\right)^2}
$$

where:

- $$N-1$$ = total number of RR intervals

- $$\overline{\text{RR}}$$ = mean RR interval over the recording

- $$\text{SDNN}$$ = standard deviation of all normal-to-normal intervals

SDNN reflects overall variability and is influenced by both short-term and long-term regulatory mechanisms.

### 5.2 RMSSD: Root Mean Square of Successive Differences

RMSSD specifically captures high-frequency variability by examining consecutive beat-to-beat differences:

$$
\text{RMSSD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-2} \sum_{i=1}^{N-2} (\text{RR}_{i+1} - \text{RR}_i)^2}
$$

where:

- $$N-2$$ = number of successive differences

- $$\text{RR}_{i+1} - \text{RR}_i$$ = difference between consecutive intervals

- $$\text{RMSSD}$$ = root mean square of those differences

RMSSD is highly sensitive to **parasympathetic nervous system** changes. It filters out slow trends to highlight rapid physiological changes that reflect vagal tone.

>[!Tip]
> RMSSD is the preferred metric for assessing short-term HRV because it is less affected by breathing patterns than frequency-domain measures and computationally straightforward.
