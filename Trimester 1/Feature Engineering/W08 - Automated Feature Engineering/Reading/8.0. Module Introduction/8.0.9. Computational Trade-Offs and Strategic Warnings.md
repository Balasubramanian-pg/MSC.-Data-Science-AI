# 8.0.9. Computational Trade-Offs and Strategic Warnings

Although Automated Feature Engineering provides significant benefits, it introduces important risks.

### 8.0.9.1. Overfitting

Automated systems can identify patterns that exist only within the training dataset.

These patterns may not generalize to future observations.

> [!Warning]
> A feature that appears highly predictive during training may simply be modeling random noise rather than a genuine underlying relationship.

Cross-validation and holdout testing remain essential.

### 8.0.9.2. Interpretability Challenges

Automatically generated features may become difficult to explain.

Example:

$$
\text{SUM(Orders)}
\times
\text{MEAN(Transaction)}
^2
$$

While predictive, such features may be difficult for business stakeholders to understand.

### 8.0.9.3. Computational Cost

Searching large feature spaces requires substantial computational resources.

Potential challenges include:

* increased CPU usage
* memory constraints
* long training times

> [!Warning]
> Automated Feature Engineering should be viewed as a force multiplier, not a replacement for exploratory data analysis and domain expertise.
