# 8.0.3. The Core Paradigm Shift

Traditional feature engineering depends heavily on human intuition.

A data scientist may examine variables and hypothesize that a specific transformation could improve predictive performance.

### 8.0.3.1. Manual Feature Engineering

Manual engineering follows a hypothesis-driven process.

Example:

* "Debt-to-Income Ratio may predict loan default."
* "Customer Tenure may predict churn."
* "Average Purchase Value may predict customer lifetime value."

The workflow typically involves:

$$
\text{Hypothesis}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Creation}
\rightarrow
\text{Model Training}
\rightarrow
\text{Evaluation}
$$

While effective, this process is slow and highly dependent on individual expertise.

### 8.0.3.2. Automated Feature Engineering

Automated approaches treat feature creation as a computational search problem.

Instead of manually designing features, algorithms systematically evaluate large numbers of transformations and combinations.

For example:

Manual approach:

$$
\frac{\text{Debt}}
{\text{Income}}
$$

Automated approach:

The algorithm may evaluate hundreds of candidate transformations such as:

$$
\frac{\text{Debt}}
{\text{Income}}
\times
\text{Age}
$$

$$
\log(\text{Debt})
$$

$$
\sqrt{\text{Income}}
$$

$$
\text{Debt}^2
$$

and many others.

> [!Tip]
> Manual feature engineering searches around human intuition. Automated feature engineering searches across a large computational feature space.
