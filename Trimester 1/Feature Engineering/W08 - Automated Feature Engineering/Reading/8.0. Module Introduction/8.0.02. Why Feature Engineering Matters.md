# 8.0.2. Why Feature Engineering Matters

The predictive power of a machine learning model often depends more on feature quality than on algorithm selection.

Consider a credit risk problem.

A dataset may contain:

* annual income
* outstanding debt
* age
* credit utilization

These raw variables may contain useful information, but their predictive value can often be increased through transformation.

For example:

$$
\text{Debt Ratio}
=================

\frac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Income}}
$$

or

$$
\text{Credit Utilization}
=========================

\frac{\text{Current Balance}}
{\text{Credit Limit}}
$$

These derived features often provide stronger predictive signals than the original variables.

The challenge is that modern datasets may contain hundreds or thousands of variables, creating an enormous number of possible feature combinations.
