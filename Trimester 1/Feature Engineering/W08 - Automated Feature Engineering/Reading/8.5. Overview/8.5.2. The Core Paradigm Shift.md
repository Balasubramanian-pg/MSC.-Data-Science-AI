# 8.5.2. The Core Paradigm Shift

Traditional feature engineering relies heavily on human intuition.

For example, a credit risk analyst might manually create:

- debt-to-income ratio
- credit utilization percentage
- average monthly expenditure
- account age

Each feature requires:

- hypothesis generation
- implementation
- validation
- iterative refinement

Automated Feature Engineering approaches the problem differently.

Rather than manually creating individual features, algorithms systematically explore a large search space of possible transformations.

The process can be viewed as:

$$
\text{Raw Data}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Search}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Generation}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Selection}
\rightarrow
\text{Model Training}
$$

This transforms feature engineering into a computational optimization problem.

>[!Tip]
> Manual feature engineering searches locally around human intuition. Automated feature engineering searches globally across thousands of potential feature combinations.
