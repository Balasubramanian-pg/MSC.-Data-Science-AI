# 8.5.5. TSFresh for Time-Series Feature Extraction

Relational data is not the only type of structured data encountered in machine learning.

Many real-world systems generate sequential observations over time.

Examples include:

- IoT sensors
- wearable devices
- manufacturing systems
- stock prices
- pharmaceutical monitoring devices

Such datasets require specialized feature extraction methods.

### 8.5.5.1. What is TSFresh?

TSFresh stands for:

**Time Series Feature Extraction Based on Scalable Hypothesis Tests**

Its objective is to automatically derive statistical characteristics from time-series signals.

Instead of feeding raw sequences directly into models, TSFresh converts signals into informative summary features.

### 8.5.5.2. Types of Extracted Features

TSFresh computes hundreds of features including:

#### Distribution-Based Features

Examples:

$$
\text{Mean}
$$

$$
\text{Variance}
$$

$$
\text{Skewness}
$$

$$
\text{Kurtosis}
$$

#### Frequency-Based Features

Using Fourier Transform methods:

$$
FFT(x)
$$

Features include:

- dominant frequencies
- spectral energy
- frequency amplitudes

#### Dependency Features

Examples include:

$$
\text{Autocorrelation}
$$

$$
\text{Partial Autocorrelation}
$$

These capture temporal dependence structures.

#### Complexity Features

Examples:

- entropy
- peak counts
- trend strength
- change points

### 8.5.5.3. Statistical Feature Filtering

TSFresh does not simply generate features.

It also performs statistical significance testing.

Generated features are tested against the target variable using methods such as:

- ANOVA
- Chi-Squared Tests
- Correlation Tests

The objective is to retain features with meaningful predictive relationships.

Conceptually:

$$
H_0:
\text{Feature has no relationship with target}
$$

$$
H_1:
\text{Feature has predictive relationship with target}
$$

Features failing significance criteria are discarded.

>[!Tip]
> TSFresh combines feature engineering and feature selection into a unified workflow.
