# 8.5.1. Introduction to Automated Feature Engineering

Feature engineering is often the most time-consuming stage of the machine learning lifecycle.

Raw data rarely contains information in a form that machine learning algorithms can directly exploit. Instead, useful predictive signals must often be extracted, transformed, aggregated, or combined into new variables called **features**.

Traditionally, this process has been performed manually by data scientists using domain expertise and iterative experimentation.

However, manual feature engineering presents several limitations:

- highly dependent on analyst expertise
- difficult to scale across projects
- prone to human bias
- expensive in terms of time and effort
- may overlook complex nonlinear relationships

Automated Feature Engineering (AFE) addresses these limitations by systematically searching for useful transformations and combinations of variables.

>[!Note]
> Automated Feature Engineering does not eliminate the need for domain expertise. Instead, it amplifies analyst productivity by discovering candidate features that can later be validated through statistical and business evaluation.

The objective is to transform feature engineering from a manual crafting process into a semi-automated search and optimization problem.
