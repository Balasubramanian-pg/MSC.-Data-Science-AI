# 2.7.10. Common Misinterpretations in Numeric Engineering

Many practitioners fall into predictable traps when engineering numeric features, leading to degraded model performance.

### Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> **"All machine learning algorithms require feature scaling."**

**Wrong.**

Tree-based models partition the feature space using ordinal threshold splits. They are entirely invariant to monotonic feature scaling and will yield the exact same splits whether the data is raw or standardized.

### Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> **"Min-Max scaling is always superior because it keeps data strictly bounded."**

**Wrong under outlier presence.**

If your dataset contains a single massive outlier, Min-Max scaling will compress 99 percent of your normal data into a microscopic range, effectively destroying their variance and rendering the feature useless for distance calculations.

### Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> **"Smoothing eliminates the need for feature scaling."**

**Wrong.**

Smoothing reduces temporal variance, but it does not alter the fundamental scale of the data. If your smoothed features exist on vastly different scales, distance-based algorithms will still be completely dominated by the feature with the largest numerical range.
