## 2.4.1. The Purpose of Smoothing

Real-world time series data often contains short-term fluctuations caused by random variation, measurement errors, seasonal effects, or isolated events. While these fluctuations are part of the observed data, they do not always represent the underlying trend that a machine learning model should learn.

Smoothing is a feature engineering technique that reduces these short-term variations to make long-term patterns more apparent. By emphasizing the underlying signal and suppressing random noise, smoothing produces features that are more stable and representative of the true behavior of the data.

This preprocessing step helps improve model generalization by reducing the likelihood of learning patterns that are driven by temporary anomalies rather than meaningful trends. Common smoothing techniques include moving averages, exponential smoothing, and rolling statistical measures, each designed to capture the underlying behavior while minimizing the impact of noise.
