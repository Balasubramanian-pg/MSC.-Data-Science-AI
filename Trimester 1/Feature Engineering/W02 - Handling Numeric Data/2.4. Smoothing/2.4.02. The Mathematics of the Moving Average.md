# 2.4.2. The Mathematics of the Moving Average

To tame this chaos, we replace the isolated observation with the collective memory of its neighbors. The Simple Moving Average computes the arithmetic mean over a fixed window of time, effectively blurring the sharp edges of the data.

Let the raw time-series observation at time $$t$$ be denoted as $$x_t$$. We define a window of size $$k$$, encompassing the current observation and the $$k-1$$ preceding observations. The smoothed value, $$\text{SMA}_t$$, is calculated as:

$$
\text{SMA}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} x_{t-i}
$$

where:

- $$\text{SMA}_t$$ = the smoothed value at time $$t$$

- $$k$$ = the number of periods in the moving window

- $$x_{t-i}$$ = the raw observation at time $$t-i$$

- $$i$$ = the lag index ranging from $$0$$ to $$k-1$$

This transformation preserves the central tendency of the data while aggressively attenuating high-frequency variance. 

Let us reiterate the core moving average formula, as it is the foundation of all temporal smoothing:

$$
\text{SMA}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} x_{t-i}
$$

With the arithmetic mean established, we must confront the reality that not all history holds equal weight.
