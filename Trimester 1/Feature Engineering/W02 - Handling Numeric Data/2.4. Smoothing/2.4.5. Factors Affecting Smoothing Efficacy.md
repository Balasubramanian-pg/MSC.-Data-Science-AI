# 2.4.5. Factors Affecting Smoothing Efficacy

The following table summarizes how different structural factors impact the performance of a smoothing technique.

| Factor | Impact on Lag | Impact on Noise Reduction |
| :--- | :---: | ---: |
| Window Size ($$k$$) | High | High |
| Smoothing Constant ($$\alpha$$) | Very High | Medium |
| Seasonality | Medium | Low |

### 2.4.1. Window Size and Lag

Increasing the window size $$k$$ or decreasing the smoothing constant $$\alpha$$ forces the model to look further back in time.

Thus:

- greater noise reduction

- increased phase lag

- delayed reaction to genuine trend shifts

### 2.4.2. Seasonality and Periodicity

If the underlying data contains strong seasonal cycles, a moving average window that does not align with the seasonal period will inadvertently smooth away the very signal you wish to capture.

This destructive interference occurs when the window size divides the seasonal period unevenly.

### 2.4.3. Outlier Sensitivity

The Simple Moving Average is highly vulnerable to extreme outliers within the window.

A single massive spike will artificially inflate the smoothed value for all $$k$$ periods, distorting the underlying trend until the outlier falls out of the window.
