# 2.6.4. Visualizing the Scaling Effects

When we apply scaling techniques to a dataset like the Breast Cancer diagnostic data, the geometric distribution of the features changes dramatically.

The original raw distribution of the Mean Radius feature exhibits a specific spread and central tendency.

When Min-Max scaling is applied, all values are strictly compressed into a bounded interval between zero and one. This preserves the original shape but forces the entire feature into a microscopic numerical range.

When Z-Score standardization is applied, the data is centered at a mean of zero with a standard deviation of one. This creates a unified mathematical space where one unit of distance represents one standard deviation of variance.

These visual transformations directly dictate how the proximity metric will behave.
