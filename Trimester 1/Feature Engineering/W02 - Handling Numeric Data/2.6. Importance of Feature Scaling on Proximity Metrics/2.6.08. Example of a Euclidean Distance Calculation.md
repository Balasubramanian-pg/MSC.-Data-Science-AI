# 2.6.8. Example of a Euclidean Distance Calculation

Suppose:

- Point $$p$$ has coordinates: Radius = 10, Texture = 0.1
- Point $$q$$ has coordinates: Radius = 20, Texture = 0.2
- Objective: Calculate the Euclidean distance between $$p$$ and $$q$$ in both raw and scaled spaces

### Step 1: Calculate Raw Distance
Substitute the raw coordinates into the Euclidean formula:

$$
d(p, q) = \sqrt{(20 - 10)^2 + (0.2 - 0.1)^2}
$$

### Step 2: Compute Raw Squared Differences
Calculate the squared differences for each dimension:

$$
d(p, q) = \sqrt{100 + 0.01} = \sqrt{100.01} \approx 10.0005
$$

### Step 3: Apply Min-Max Scaling
Assume Radius ranges from 0 to 100, and Texture ranges from 0 to 1. The scaled coordinates for $$p$$ become Radius = 0.1, Texture = 0.1. The scaled coordinates for $$q$$ become Radius = 0.2, Texture = 0.2.

### Step 4: Calculate Scaled Distance
Substitute the scaled coordinates into the Euclidean formula:

$$
d(p, q)_{\text{scaled}} = \sqrt{(0.2 - 0.1)^2 + (0.2 - 0.1)^2}
$$

### Step 5: Final Result
Compute the final scaled distance:

$$
d(p, q)_{\text{scaled}} = \sqrt{0.01 + 0.01} = \sqrt{0.02} \approx \mathbf{0.141}
$$

The distance dropped from 10.0005 to 0.141, and the contribution of the Texture feature is now perfectly balanced with the Radius feature.

Execution is only half the battle; understanding the variables that impact transformation efficacy is what separates robust models from fragile ones.
