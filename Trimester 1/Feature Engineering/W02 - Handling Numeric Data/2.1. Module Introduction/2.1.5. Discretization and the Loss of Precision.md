# 2.1.5. Discretization and the Loss of Precision

Sometimes, the continuous nature of numeric data is a liability rather than an asset. Continuous variables can cause linear models to overfit to minor fluctuations.

Discretization, or binning, converts continuous variables into discrete categorical buckets. By grouping ages into decades or incomes into brackets, we force the model to ignore microscopic noise and focus on macroscopic trends.

However, this comes at a cost. Binning destroys the granular variance within each bucket. It is a deliberate sacrifice of precision for the sake of robustness.

The following table explains the primary preprocessing techniques and their specific geometric impacts on the data.

| Technique | Primary Objective | Impact on Distribution |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Min-Max Scaling | Bound values within a strict range | Preserves original shape |
| Standardization | Center data with unit variance | Preserves original shape |
| Log Transformation | Compress extreme right-tail outliers | Reduces right-skew |
| Discretization | Group continuous values into buckets | Destroys intra-bucket variance |
