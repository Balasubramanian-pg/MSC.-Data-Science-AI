# 2.2.3. Practical Implementation in Banking Data

When predicting term deposit subscriptions in a banking dataset, the `age` attribute is a continuous variable ranging from late teens to late nineties. 

A linear model assumes that the probability of subscribing increases by the exact same margin for a 20-year-old and an 80-year-old. We know this is false. The relationship is highly nonlinear. 

By applying equal width binning, we group the population into "Young," "Adult," "Mature," and "Senior." By applying equal frequency binning, we divide the customers into quartiles based on their actual population density. We can also apply domain-specific binary flagging, such as creating a boolean indicator for customers over 60.

The following table compares the primary binning strategies based on their structural impact on the data.

| Strategy | Boundary Definition | Impact on Skewed Data |
| :--- | :---: | ---: |
| Equal Width | Fixed mathematical intervals | Creates empty or massive bins |
| Equal Frequency | Fixed number of records per bin | Balances bin populations perfectly |
| Domain-Specific | Business logic thresholds | Aligns with real-world categories |
