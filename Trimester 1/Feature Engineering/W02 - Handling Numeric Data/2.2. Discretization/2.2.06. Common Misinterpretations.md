# 2.2.6. Common Misinterpretations

Many practitioners fall into predictable traps when discretizing continuous variables, leading to degraded model performance.

### 6.1 Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> **"Binning always improves model performance by reducing noise."**

Wrong. 

If the underlying relationship between the feature and the target is strictly linear and monotonic, binning will artificially introduce step-functions and degrade predictive accuracy. You are destroying signal to kill noise.

### 6.2 Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> **"Equal width binning is the safest default strategy."**

Wrong under skewed distributions. 

If your data follows a power-law distribution, equal width binning will result in 99% of your data crammed into the first bin, rendering the rest of the bins statistically useless. Always check the distribution first.

### 6.3 Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> **"Discretization eliminates the need for feature scaling."**

Not necessarily. 

While the binned categories are now discrete, if you apply target encoding or leave the bins as ordinal integers, the model might still assume a linear progression across the bins. You must explicitly treat them as categorical variables.
