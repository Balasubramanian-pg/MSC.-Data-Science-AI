# 2.2.1. The Philosophy of Discretization

Look, continuous data is an illusion of infinite precision. We measure age as 24.53 years, but human behavior does not shift meaningfully at that exact decimal. Discretization, or binning, is the act of stripping away this false precision to reveal the underlying categorical reality. 

By transforming a continuous numerical variable into a set of discrete intervals, we simplify complex data, identify nonlinear relationships, and reduce the impact of noise. If we feed raw continuous variables into a linear model, it assumes a constant rate of change across the entire spectrum. Binning shatters that assumption, allowing the model to learn distinct behaviors for different segments of the population.
