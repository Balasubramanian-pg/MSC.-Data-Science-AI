# 6.1.8. Conclusions

Canny edge detection transforms raw pixel intensities into structured boundary maps that capture object geometry while suppressing noise.

### 8.1 Anatomy of the Canny Pipeline

The structure of every Canny edge detection follows:

$$
\text{Edge Map} = \text{Smoothing} \rightarrow \text{Gradient} \rightarrow \text{Thinning} \rightarrow \text{Thresholding} \rightarrow \text{Linking}
$$

- **Smoothing**: Gaussian convolution suppresses high-frequency noise

- **Gradient**: Sobel operators compute magnitude and direction of intensity change

- **Thinning**: Non-maximum suppression reduces edges to one-pixel width

- **Thresholding**: Double thresholds classify edge strength

- **Linking**: Hysteresis connects weak edges to strong ones, forming continuous contours

### 8.2 Parameter Selection Guide

| Parameter | Symbol | Typical Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian sigma | $$\sigma$$ | 0.5 to 2.0 | Scale of detected edges |
| High threshold | $$T_{\text{high}}$$ | 100 to 300 | Minimum strong edge strength |
| Low threshold | $$T_{\text{low}}$$ | 50 to 150 | Minimum weak edge strength |

### 8.3 Critical Principles

Understanding the multi-stage nature of edge detection is essential:

- **Noise reduction precedes differentiation**: Smoothing must occur before gradient calculation to prevent noise amplification

- **Thinning preserves localization**: Non-maximum suppression ensures edges are precisely located, not spread across pixels

- **Hysteresis enables continuity**: Weak edges connected to strong ones are preserved, allowing detection of faint but structurally important boundaries

>[!Tip]
> "The best edge detectors combine gradient sensitivity with structural coherence. Canny's five-stage pipeline optimizes for detection, localization, and minimal response—three criteria that simpler methods cannot simultaneously satisfy."
