# 6.1.5. Practical Implementation Considerations

### 5.1 Threshold Selection

The high and low thresholds are the primary tunable parameters:

| Threshold Ratio | Effect | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| High $$T_{\text{high}}$$, Low $$T_{\text{low}}$$ | Fewer, prominent edges only | Clean images with strong boundaries |
| Low $$T_{\text{high}}$$, Low $$T_{\text{low}}$$ | More edges, more detail | Complex scenes with fine textures |
| Adaptive thresholds | Dynamic per-image | Variable lighting conditions |

A common heuristic is:

$$
T_{\text{high}} = \text{median}(M_{\text{nms}}) \times k, \quad T_{\text{low}} = \frac{T_{\text{high}}}{2 \text{ or } 3}
$$

where $$k$$ is a scaling factor (typically 0.66 to 1.33).

### 5.2 Gaussian Sigma Selection

The smoothing parameter $$\sigma$$ controls the scale of edges detected:

| $$\sigma$$ Value | Edge Scale | Noise Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| Small ($$\sigma < 1$$) | Fine details | High |
| Medium ($$\sigma \approx 1.4$$) | Balanced | Moderate |
| Large ($$\sigma > 2$$) | Coarse structures | Low |

Larger $$\sigma$$ suppresses fine edges but improves noise robustness.

### 5.3 Preprocessing Requirements

>[!Warning]
> Always ensure the input image is converted to grayscale and normalized before Canny detection. Color channels introduce redundant information and may destabilize gradient calculations.

Standard preprocessing:

1. Convert to grayscale: $$I_{\text{gray}} = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$$

2. Normalize to $$[0, 1]$$or$$[0, 255]$$

3. Apply Gaussian smoothing (built into Canny or performed separately)
