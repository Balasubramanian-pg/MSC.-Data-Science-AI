# 6.1.7. Common Misinterpretations

### Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> "Higher thresholds always produce better edges."

Wrong. Excessively high thresholds eliminate genuine weak edges, breaking continuous contours. The optimal threshold depends on image content and must balance completeness against noise.

### Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> "Canny edges are invariant to illumination changes."

Wrong. While Gaussian smoothing provides some robustness, extreme illumination variation can shift gradient magnitudes across threshold boundaries. Preprocessing (histogram equalization) may be necessary.

### Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> "Edge detection alone is sufficient for object recognition."

Wrong. Edges provide boundary information but no region labels, textures, or semantic context. Object recognition requires additional processing: contour analysis, feature descriptors, and classification.
