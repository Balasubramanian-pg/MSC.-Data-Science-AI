# 6.2.4. Shi-Tomasi Corner Detection

Shi-Tomasi improves upon Harris by using a more direct and stable criterion.

### 4.1 The Minimum Eigenvalue Criterion

Instead of the Harris response formula, Shi-Tomasi uses:

$$
R_{\text{ST}} = \min(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)
$$

where:

- $$R_{\text{ST}}$$ = Shi-Tomasi corner response

- $$\lambda_1, \lambda_2$$= eigenvalues of the structure tensor$$M$$

- $$\min(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$$ = the smaller eigenvalue

A corner is confirmed if and only if:

$$
\min(\lambda_1, \lambda_2) > T
$$

where $$T$$ is a threshold.

### 4.2 Why Shi-Tomasi Is More Robust

The Harris response $$R = \det(M) - k \cdot \text{tr}(M)^2$$ depends on the empirical constant$$k$$. The choice of $$k$$ affects the balance between corner and edge classification.

Shi-Tomasi eliminates this parameter entirely. By using $$\min(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$$, the algorithm directly tests the defining property of a corner: both eigenvalues must be large. The smaller eigenvalue is the bottleneck; if it is large, the point is unambiguously a corner.

>[!Tip]
> Shi-Tomasi is generally preferred in modern pipelines because it is less sensitive to parameter tuning and produces more repeatable detections across similar images.
