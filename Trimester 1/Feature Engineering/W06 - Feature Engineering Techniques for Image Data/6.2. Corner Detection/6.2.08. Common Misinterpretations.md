# 6.2.8. Common Misinterpretations

### Interpretation 1

>[!Warning]
> "A high Harris response always indicates a strong corner."

Wrong. The Harris response can be positive for edge-like structures with moderately large eigenvalues. Shi-Tomasi's minimum eigenvalue criterion is more discriminating because it requires both eigenvalues to be large.

### Interpretation 2

>[!Warning]
> "More detected corners always improve matching performance."

Wrong. Excessive corners increase computational cost and may include unstable points from texture or noise. The quality level and minimum distance parameters should be tuned to retain only the most salient and spatially distributed corners.

### Interpretation 3

>[!Warning]
> "Corner detection is rotation invariant."

Wrong. While corners are more robust to rotation than edges, the exact response values and detected positions may shift under rotation due to discretization effects. For true rotation invariance, additional descriptors (like SIFT or ORB) are required.
