# 6.2.3. Harris Corner Detection

Harris Corner Detection formalizes the intuition that corners produce large intensity changes when a small window is shifted in any direction.

### 3.1 The Intuition

Consider a small window $$W$$centered at pixel$$(x, y)$$. Shift this window by a small amount $$(\Delta x, \Delta y)$$. The change in intensity is:

$$
E(\Delta x, \Delta y) = \sum_{(u,v) \in W} w(u,v) \left[ I(u+\Delta x, v+\Delta y) - I(u,v) \right]^2
$$

where:

- $$E(\Delta x, \Delta y)$$ = sum of squared intensity differences after shift

- $$w(u,v)$$ = window weight (typically Gaussian or uniform)

- $$I(u,v)$$= intensity at pixel$$(u,v)$$ within the window

For small shifts, we approximate using a first-order Taylor expansion:

$$
I(u+\Delta x, v+\Delta y) \approx I(u,v) + I_x(u,v)\Delta x + I_y(u,v)\Delta y
$$

where:

- $$I_x(u,v)$$= partial derivative of intensity with respect to$$x$$

- $$I_y(u,v)$$= partial derivative of intensity with respect to$$y$$

Substituting:

$$
E(\Delta x, \Delta y) \approx \sum_{(u,v) \in W} w(u,v) \left[ I_x(u,v)\Delta x + I_y(u,v)\Delta y \right]^2
$$

This can be written in matrix form:

$$
E(\Delta x, \Delta y) \approx \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x & \Delta y \end{bmatrix} M \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x \\ \Delta y \end{bmatrix}
$$

where $$M$$ is the **structure tensor** (autocorrelation matrix):

$$
M = \sum_{(u,v) \in W} w(u,v) \begin{bmatrix} I_x^2 & I_x I_y \\ I_x I_y & I_y^2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & C \\ C & B \end{bmatrix}
$$

where:

- $$A = \sum w(u,v) I_x^2$$ = sum of squared horizontal gradients

- $$B = \sum w(u,v) I_y^2$$ = sum of squared vertical gradients

- $$C = \sum w(u,v) I_x I_y$$ = sum of cross-gradient products

### 3.2 Interpreting the Structure Tensor

The eigenvalues of $$M$$ reveal the local structure:

| Eigenvalue Pattern | Interpretation | Visual Category |
|---|---|---|
| $$\lambda_1 \approx 0, \lambda_2 \approx 0$$ | No intensity change in any direction | Flat region |
| $$\lambda_1 \gg 0, \lambda_2 \approx 0$$ | Large change in one direction only | Edge |
| $$\lambda_1 \gg 0, \lambda_2 \gg 0$$ | Large change in both directions | Corner |

where:

- $$\lambda_1, \lambda_2$$= eigenvalues of the structure tensor$$M$$

- $$\lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2 \geq 0$$(both eigenvalues are non-negative because$$M$$ is symmetric positive semi-definite)

>[!Note]
> The structure tensor transforms the problem of corner detection into an eigenvalue analysis. A corner is defined mathematically as a point where both eigenvalues are large.

### 3.3 The Harris Response Function

Rather than computing eigenvalues explicitly (computationally expensive), Harris proposed a response function:

$$
R = \det(M) - k \cdot \text{tr}(M)^2
$$

where:

- $$R$$ = Harris corner response score

- $$\det(M) = AB - C^2 = \lambda_1 \lambda_2$$= determinant of$$M$$

- $$\text{tr}(M) = A + B = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$$= trace of$$M$$

- $$k$$= empirical constant (typically$$0.04$$ to $$0.06$$)

The response $$R$$ classifies local structure:

| Response Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| $$R \gg 0$$ | Corner (both eigenvalues large) |
| $$R \ll 0$$ | Edge (one eigenvalue large, one small) |
| $$|R| \approx 0$$ | Flat region (both eigenvalues small) |

### 3.4 Algorithm Steps

The Harris algorithm proceeds through five steps:

**Step 1: Compute Image Gradients**

$$
I_x = \frac{\partial I}{\partial x}, \quad I_y = \frac{\partial I}{\partial y}
$$

using Sobel operators or finite differences.

**Step 2: Build the Structure Tensor**

At each pixel, compute:

$$
M = \begin{bmatrix} \sum w I_x^2 & \sum w I_x I_y \\ \sum w I_x I_y & \sum w I_y^2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

where the sums are over the local window $$W$$.

**Step 3: Calculate Corner Response**

$$
R = \det(M) - k \cdot \text{tr}(M)^2
$$

**Step 4: Threshold the Response**

Identify candidate corners where:

$$
R > T
$$

where $$T$$is a threshold (often expressed as a fraction of the maximum response, e.g.,$$T = 0.01 \cdot R_{\max}$$).

**Step 5: Apply Non-Maximum Suppression**

Within each local neighborhood, retain only the pixel with maximum $$R$$. Suppress all other candidates.
