# 6.2.5. Example: Computing Harris and Shi-Tomasi Responses

Suppose:

- A $$3 \times 3$$window centered at pixel$$(x, y)$$

- Gradient values within the window:

| $$I_x$$|$$I_y$$ |
|---|---|
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 2 | 2 |

### Step 1: Compute Structure Tensor Components

With uniform weights ($$w = 1$$ for all pixels):

$$
A = \sum I_x^2 = 4 + 9 + 1 + 4 + 16 + 1 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 52
$$

$$
B = \sum I_y^2 = 1 + 4 + 1 + 9 + 4 + 4 + 1 + 9 + 4 = 37
$$

$$
C = \sum I_x I_y = 2 + 6 + 1 + 6 + 8 + 2 + 2 + 9 + 4 = 40
$$

### Step 2: Form the Structure Tensor

$$
M = \begin{bmatrix} 52 & 40 \\ 40 & 37 \end{bmatrix}
$$

### Step 3: Compute Eigenvalues

The characteristic equation is:

$$
\det(M - \lambda I) = (52 - \lambda)(37 - \lambda) - 40^2 = 0
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 89\lambda + 1924 - 1600 = 0
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 89\lambda + 324 = 0
$$

Using the quadratic formula:

$$
\lambda = \frac{89 \pm \sqrt{89^2 - 4(324)}}{2} = \frac{89 \pm \sqrt{7921 - 1296}}{2} = \frac{89 \pm \sqrt{6625}}{2}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{89 \pm 81.39}{2}
$$

$$
\lambda_1 = \frac{89 + 81.39}{2} = 85.20, \quad \lambda_2 = \frac{89 - 81.39}{2} = 3.81
$$

### Step 4: Compute Harris Response

With $$k = 0.04$$:

$$
\det(M) = 52 \times 37 - 40^2 = 1924 - 1600 = 324
$$

$$
\text{tr}(M) = 52 + 37 = 89
$$

$$
R = 324 - 0.04 \times 89^2 = 324 - 0.04 \times 7921 = 324 - 316.84 = 7.16
$$

Since $$R > 0$$, this point is classified as a corner.

### Step 5: Compute Shi-Tomasi Response

$$
R_{\text{ST}} = \min(\lambda_1, \lambda_2) = \min(85.20, 3.81) = 3.81
$$

Since both eigenvalues are positive and the minimum is non-negligible, Shi-Tomasi also classifies this as a corner. The small value of $$\lambda_2$$relative to$$\lambda_1$$ suggests a weak corner or an edge-like structure.
