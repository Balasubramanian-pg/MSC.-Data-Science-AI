# 6.2.1. From Edges to Points of Interest

Edge detection identifies boundaries, but boundaries alone do not fully describe an image's structure.

A corner is a point where two or more edges intersect. Unlike an edge pixel, which exhibits intensity variation in only one direction, a corner exhibits significant intensity variation in **multiple directions**. This multi-directional variation makes corners uniquely informative for tasks like image matching, object tracking, and scene reconstruction.

The intensity at a pixel $$(x, y)$$ in a grayscale image is:

$$
I(x, y), \quad x = 1, 2, \dots, W; \quad y = 1, 2, \dots, H
$$

where:

- $$I(x, y)$$= intensity at pixel coordinate$$(x, y)$$

- $$W$$ = image width in pixels

- $$H$$ = image height in pixels

An edge is a locus of points where the gradient magnitude is locally maximal in one direction. A corner is a point where the gradient is large in at least two independent directions. The fundamental difference is directional variation.

>[!Note]
> Corners are more informative than edges for many vision tasks because they are localizable, distinctive, and invariant to small viewpoint changes.
